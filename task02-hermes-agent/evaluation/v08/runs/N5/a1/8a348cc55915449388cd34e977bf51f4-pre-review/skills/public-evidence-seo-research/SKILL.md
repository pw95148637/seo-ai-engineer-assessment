# Public-evidence competitor SEO research

## Workflow
- For review-feedback-only requests, distinguish a supplied adjudication from a request to re-audit: when the user supplies a past example solely to establish a future standard, update the relevant evidence gate without reopening the website or claiming fresh verification. This preserves the requested scope and avoids treating historical quotations as new research tasks.
1. Start with `research_status` when using the research suite. Apply scoped, unexpired lessons; read successful current-run controller verifications with `research_evidence` instead of refetching. Note budgets and stop conditions.
2. State falsifiable hypotheses before tool selection: which pages target the terms, how internal links connect them, whether independent sites really mention/link to the competitor, and what gaps our own site could test. Never assume rankings or strategy effectiveness.
3. Batch independent homepage fetch and initial searches. Give every fetch a purpose naming the hypothesis and possible counterevidence. Follow discovered links to the hub, core generic/brand pages, and a contrasting sample; do not accumulate URLs as a proxy for quality.
4. Inspect title, H1, description, canonical, robots, visible sections, authorship, dates, sources, FAQ, internal anchors, and commercial destinations. Follow one relevant category/filter link when testing a hub: compare its actual listing, title/description, and canonical with the unfiltered hub before treating it as a separate search landing page. A changed listing can serve navigation while canonicalizing to the hub; neither configuration proves search-engine adoption. Separate metadata configuration from actual indexation, rankings, rich-result eligibility, and performance.
5. Test content-quality judgments against another brand or discovered long-tail page. Compare substantive answers, not section counts. Check whether question-shaped keywords are incorrectly treated as platform/entity names in the actual text; distinguish this demonstrated intent error from mere template similarity. When a hub excerpt suggests a consequential intent error, prioritize fetching that linked destination over adding more generic samples: confirm whether the body repeats the error or corrects it before elevating the finding to article-level criticism. Record conflicting figures with conditions and measurement endpoints (for example, withdrawal notification versus funds received, first versus subsequent withdrawal); figures without comparable samples are unexplained discrepancies, not proof of fabrication. Shared structure supports a template inference, not automatic AI-generation, penalty, or cannibalization claims.
6. Search domain and brand variants for external evidence within the search limit. Reserve a query for the site's own displayed brand rather than exhausting the budget on the reviewed operator's brand: operator-name matches may identify unrelated review sites, not mentions of the competitor. Check collisions with tickers, product codes, or unrelated entities. Snippets identify candidates, not verified endorsements. After empty results change strategy rather than repeatedly tightening the same query; no results does not prove no backlinks.
7. Read a few relevant search-listed external pages with `research_offpage`; one ambiguous candidate can be checked to establish a false positive. Record actual target URL, anchor, rel, context, and relevance. For a verified false positive with no target link, explicitly mark target anchor and rel as not applicable and describe the unrelated context; do not turn an unrelated occurrence of the domain string into a brand mention. Treat `outbound_hosts` as outgoing links only. A shared tracked destination can support a funnel inference, not ownership, commission, or incoming-link claims. Separate off-page observations, inferences, and unknowns.
8. Revise hypotheses explicitly when counterevidence arrives. Respect robots and access denials without bypass or repeated retries. Stop at budget or after two steps yield no new evidence; report narrow coverage rather than inventing performance or backlink data.
9. Rank actions for OUR site, not the competitor. Start with our inventory, legal/compliance scope, and authorized baseline data; label missing dependencies. Prefer a small evidence-rich core-page test before mass long-tail publishing. Separate implementation cost, evidence quality, and unknown return. State evidence confidence independently of effort estimates: a directly verified competitor defect can justify a test without proving search demand, low competition, or likely ranking gains. Define measurements using our authorized indexation, query-to-page mapping, impressions/clicks, and relevant citations—not invented uplift.
10. Before `research_finish`, check every observation/inference against actual current-run content. Failed retrieval/search evidence supports access claims only; treat blank search-result rows without a usable URL as no usable candidate, not as a verified source. Include on-page and off-page claims with kind, topic, and evidence IDs; use unknowns when coverage is insufficient. Treat report acceptance as schema/integrity validation, not semantic endorsement. Return conclusions, counterevidence, limitations, prioritized actions, and direct source links in the user's language.

## Standing evidence gates
- Keep competitor public evidence separate from our private analytics and proposed changes; never imply access to competitor Search Console or analytics.
- Distinguish page-reported article totals and claimed firsthand testing from independently verified quantity and quality.
- Treat editorial-team or organization bylines only as team attribution, not as named authors, identifiable author information, demonstrated expertise, or professional endorsement. Claim a named author only when the evidence identifies an individual; assess credentials separately. Keep compound claims granular so practical details or update labels do not lend unsupported credibility to authorship claims.
- Bound negative findings to inspected pages and extraction: absent from sampled visible content does not mean absent across the site. Attribute intent errors found in hub/card excerpts to those excerpts, not to unread destination articles; an excerpt is evidence of its own wording, not proof of the full article's quality.
- Do not infer no backlinks, low authority, domain transfer, or successful link building from sparse search results.
- For gambling reviews, distinguish transport encryption from operator legitimacy or payout safety. Do not endorse unverified safety or manufacture transaction tests. Favor sourced claims, commercial disclosure, jurisdiction review, and risk information without inducing deposits or sensitive-document submission.
- Keep progress updates brief and decision-focused; final tables connect evidence to interpretation and actions rather than replaying tool calls.
